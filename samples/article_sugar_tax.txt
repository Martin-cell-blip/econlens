The city council will vote next month on a 20% excise tax on sugar-sweetened
beverages, a measure health officials say could cut consumption by up to 15%
among teenagers. Supporters point to hospital data showing obesity-related
treatment costs reached $48 million last year, up from $31 million five years
ago. "The price of these drinks does not reflect what they cost society," said
the deputy mayor.

Beverage industry representatives warn the tax will hit low-income households
hardest and could cost 300 retail jobs. Small shop owners note that cross-border
shopping in the neighbouring county, which has no such tax, could blunt the
policy's effect. A similar levy introduced in a nearby state saw sales of taxed
drinks fall 22% in the first year, while sales of untaxed juices and bottled
water rose 9%. Revenue from the proposed tax, estimated at $12 million a year,
would be earmarked for school sports programmes.
